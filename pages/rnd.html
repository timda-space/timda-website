Research & Development keeps the station's protocols humming. Please do not touch the protocols.

The specs

Everything TIMDA does is written down, small enough to read in an evening, and open to anyone to implement:

  • TIMDA-1: The _timda finger records: the DNS records a Section publishes: a core record, one record per key, and optional airlock, charter, setting, button, banner and inbox records.
  • TIMDA-2: Signed statements: how a Section speaks, signed with ssh-keygen and checked against its DNS. Docking, the Board, and where hubs take statements.
  • TIMDA-3: Inboxes: signed mail between Sections, held by a hub until it's collected, and sealed with age if the recipient asks.

TIMDA-1 and TIMDA-2 are stable (1.0): they'll only grow in ways that leave today's records and signatures working. TIMDA-3 is a draft, working on this hub and still open to change from what its first users find.

They're published under CC BY 4.0: copy them, translate them, build on them, with credit to timda.space.

The tools

Nothing here needs special software. dig, ssh-keygen, age and curl do it all, and every page that asks you to sign something shows how. For convenience there's timda, a command-line tool with no dependencies beyond Node 20:

timda finger section72.net      who is this Section?
timda plan --from section.toml  every record to publish, checked
timda dock                      ask the hub to list you
timda mail                      collect your mail, checked
timda mail send section76.net   write to another Section

Its parts also run here in your browser, with no JavaScript sent to you: the beacon test is timda finger, and the record planner is timda plan. The tool's code is MIT-licensed, and the hub's is AGPL-3.0, so you can run your own.

Out of character

The protocol is the product, not this site. Hubs are post offices, not authorities: a Section's identity is its domain and its key, and anyone can check any signature against DNS without trusting a hub at all. If you'd rather write TIMDA in Go, Python or shell, the specs are all you need.
